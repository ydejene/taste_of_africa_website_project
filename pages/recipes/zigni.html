Eritrean Zigni (Tsebhi Sga)

Eritrea’s national spicy beef stew: tender morsels of beef slow-simmered in an intensely aromatic tomato, onion, and berbere sauce enriched with spiced ghee.

Prep Time 25 mins
Cook Time 1 hr 30 mins
Total Time 1 hr 55 mins
Servings 5 servings
Cultural Significance

A Heritage of Resilient Flavors

In Eritrean households, Zigni (also known as Tsebhi Sga) is the quintessential celebration stew. While closely related to Ethiopian beef wat, Eritrean Zigni is famous for its vibrant incorporation of puréed fresh plum tomatoes alongside generous amounts of berbere, creating a brighter, tangy depth beneath the fiery chili heat. Slow cooking ensures that the beef becomes fork-tender while the onions disintegrate completely into a smooth, glossy red sauce. Served atop tangy sour injera (taita), it is an enduring symbol of hospitality and togetherness.

Ingredients

Measured for 5 hearty servings.

  • Boneless stewing beef (chuck or shank), cubed 1 kg
  • Red onions, very finely diced 4 medium
  • Eritrean berbere spice blend ¼ cup
  • Ripe plum tomatoes, puréed 4 medium
  • Tomato paste 2 tbsp
  • Tesmi (Eritrean spiced clarified butter) or ghee ⅓ cup
  • Garlic cloves, minced 5 cloves
  • Fresh ginger, finely grated 1½ tbsp
  • Ground korarima (Ethiopian cardamom) ½ tsp
  • Beef bone broth or warm water 1 cup
  • Coarse sea salt to taste
  • Fresh taita (injera) or crusty flatbread for serving

Preparation Instructions

Click each step to expand details and cooking notes.

Step 1: Dry Caramelize the Red Onions

Place the finely diced red onions into a dry, heavy-bottomed Dutch oven over medium-low heat. Cook slowly for 30–35 minutes without adding oil, stirring frequently. When the onions release their water, cover the pot briefly to steam them tender, then uncover and cook until they reduce into a rich, golden paste.

Step 2: Add Spiced Butter (Tesmi) & Berbere

Stir the tesmi (spiced clarified butter) into the softened onions. Once melted, add the berbere spice blend. Stir constantly on low heat for 10–12 minutes, allowing the chili, fenugreek, and ginger in the berbere to bloom into a fragrant, brick-red sauce.

Step 3: Integrate Tomatoes and Aromatics

Add the tomato paste, puréed fresh plum tomatoes, minced garlic, and grated ginger. Stir thoroughly to incorporate with the berbere base. Cook uncovered for 10 minutes until the tomato sauce thickens and deepens in color.

Step 4: Brown and Braise the Beef

Add the cubed beef to the pot, turning each piece to coat thoroughly in the rich sauce. Season with sea salt. Pour in 1 cup of warm beef bone broth. Cover tightly, reduce the heat to the lowest setting, and simmer gently for 1 hour to 1 hour and 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the beef is exceptionally tender.

Step 5: Finish with Korarima & Rest

Sprinkle the ground korarima across the stew and stir gently. Simmer uncovered for 5 minutes until the gravy turns glossy and rich. Turn off the heat and allow the Zigni to rest for 10 minutes before serving so the flavors settle.

Step 6: Serve with Fresh Taita

Spread fresh sourdough taita (injera) over a shared platter. Spoon the steaming Zigni onto the center, allowing the tangy bread to absorb the rich berbere sauce. Enjoy communally with family and friends.
