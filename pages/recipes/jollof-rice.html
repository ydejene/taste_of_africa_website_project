Nigerian Party Jollof Rice

The undisputed king of celebratory feasts across West Africa, distinguished by its signature smoky aroma, deeply seasoned tomato-pepper reduction, and tender grain separation.

Prep Time 25 mins
Cook Time 50 mins
Total Time 1 hr 15 mins
Servings 6 servings
Cultural Significance

The Soul of Nigerian Celebrations

No Nigerian wedding, birthday, or festive gathering is complete without "party jollof." Derived historically from the Wolof people of Senegambia, this beloved dish evolved in Nigeria into a distinct culinary artform. The secret to authentic Nigerian jollof lies in the reduction of roasted red bell peppers (tatashe), ripe plum tomatoes, scotch bonnets (atarodo), and red onions, followed by a controlled steaming process under foil where the bottom layer slightly chars to infuse the entire pot with a rich, smoky essence.

Ingredients

Measured for 6 generous servings.

  • Parboiled long-grain rice 3 cups
  • Ripe plum tomatoes 6 medium
  • Red bell peppers (tatashe) 3 large
  • Scotch bonnet peppers (atarodo) 2 peppers
  • Red onions, divided 2 medium
  • Double concentrated tomato paste 100 g
  • Vegetable oil ½ cup
  • Rich chicken or beef stock 2½ cups
  • Dried thyme & curry powder 1 tbsp each
  • Dried bay leaves 3 leaves
  • Sea salt and stock cubes to taste
  • Butter for finishing 2 tbsp

Preparation Instructions

Click each step to expand details and cooking notes.

Step 1: Blend & Reduce Pepper Base (Obé)

Roughly chop the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnets, and one red onion. Blend with minimal water until smooth. Pour the blended purée into a heavy saucepan and boil over medium-high heat for 12–15 minutes until excess water evaporates, leaving a thick, concentrated pepper paste.

Step 2: Fry the Stew Base

Heat ½ cup of vegetable oil in a large Dutch oven or cast-iron pot over medium heat. Slice the second red onion and fry gently until fragrant and translucent. Add the tomato paste and fry for 5–7 minutes, stirring constantly to remove raw acidity. Pour in the boiled pepper purée, dried thyme, curry powder, and bay leaves. Fry for 15–20 minutes until the oil separates to the top.

Step 3: Parboil & Wash the Rice

Thoroughly wash the parboiled rice in warm water multiple times until the water runs completely clear, removing excess starch that prevents clumpiness. Drain the washed rice completely in a fine-mesh colander.

Step 4: Combine Rice, Seasoning & Stock

Pour the rich chicken or beef stock into the fried tomato stew. Season with salt and crushed stock cubes to taste; the liquid should taste slightly more seasoned than desired, as the rice will absorb flavor. Add the drained rice and stir thoroughly to ensure every grain is coated in sauce. The liquid level should just graze the surface of the rice.

Step 5: Steam Low & Slow under Foil

Cover the pot tightly with aluminum foil or parchment paper before sealing with the heavy lid. Turn the heat down to low. Allow the rice to cook in trapped steam for 25–30 minutes without opening the lid. Steam is what cooks jollof rice tenderly, not excess water.

Step 6: The Signature Smoky Finish

Once the liquid is absorbed and the grains are tender, remove the foil. Drop in 2 tablespoons of butter and optional sliced fresh tomatoes or onions. Increase the heat to medium-low for 3–5 minutes, allowing the bottom layer of rice to lightly scorch. Stir gently with a wooden paddle from the bottom up to distribute the smoky party aroma throughout the pot. Serve hot with fried plantains.
