Malagasy Romazava

Madagascar’s national dish: a restorative, fragrant clear meat stew simmered with leafy greens, ginger, fresh tomatoes, and the tingling botanical note of anamalao (brède mafane).

Prep Time 20 mins
Cook Time 1 hr 15 mins
Total Time 1 hr 35 mins
Servings 5 servings
Cultural Significance

The Botanical Soul of Madagascar

Romazava holds a unique place in the culinary tapestry of Madagascar, where Austronesian seafaring roots intermingled with East African agricultural traditions. The hallmark of Romazava is its use of indigenous greens, specifically anamalao (known in French as brède mafane or toothache plant). The flower buds and leaves contain spilanthol, which imparts a gentle, invigorating tingling and mouth-cooling sensation on the palate that enhances the richness of braised zebu beef. Traditionally enjoyed with white rice and fiery rougail condiment, Romazava is both a royal dish of ancient Merina monarchs and a treasured comfort food in every Malagasy home.

Ingredients

Measured for 5 comforting servings.

  • Braising beef (chuck or brisket), cut into bite-sized cubes 800 g
  • Fresh ginger root, peeled and grated 2 tbsp
  • Garlic cloves, crushed 4 cloves
  • Medium yellow onions, chopped 2 medium
  • Fresh plum tomatoes, diced 3 medium
  • Anamalao (brède mafane / watercress substitute) 200 g
  • Fresh spinach or mustard greens (brèdes de Chine) 200 g
  • Vegetable oil 2 tbsp
  • Water or light meat stock 4 cups
  • Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste
  • Steamed Jasmine or white rice for serving
  • Sakay (Malagasy chili paste) or tomato rougail optional

Preparation Instructions

Click each step to expand details and cooking notes.

Step 1: Sear the Beef

Heat 2 tablespoons of vegetable oil in a heavy Dutch oven or stockpot over medium-high heat. Season the beef cubes lightly with salt. Brown the meat in batches for 5–7 minutes until caramelized on the edges, developing a foundational savory fond at the bottom of the pot.

Step 2: Sauté Aromatics and Tomatoes

Reduce the heat to medium. Add the chopped onions, grated ginger, and crushed garlic to the pot. Sauté for 4–5 minutes until fragrant and translucent. Add the diced tomatoes, stirring thoroughly to deglaze the bottom of the pot and dissolve the browned meat juices.

Step 3: Simmer the Broth

Pour in 4 cups of water or light stock. Bring to a boil, then immediately turn the heat down to low. Cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid and simmer gently for 50 minutes to 1 hour, until the beef is tender and the broth has absorbed the essence of ginger and tomato.

Step 4: Wash and Prepare the Greens

Thoroughly wash the anamalao leaves (including small yellow buds) and the mixed mustard or spinach greens in cold water. Trim any tough stems. Coarsely chop the leaves into bite-sized clusters.

Step 5: Fold in the Greens

Uncover the pot and gently fold in the mixed leafy greens. Do not overcook; simmer uncovered on low heat for only 8–10 minutes until the greens are tender and wilted into the broth but retain their vibrant emerald color. Season with salt and black pepper to taste.

Step 6: Traditional Malagasy Service

Serve the steaming Romazava in deep bowls with plenty of its aromatic, tingling broth. Accompany with generous mounds of plain steamed white rice (vary) and spicy tomato rougail or sakay chili sauce on the side.
