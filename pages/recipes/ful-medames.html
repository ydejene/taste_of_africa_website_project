Sudanese Ful Medames

The heart of Sudanese daily dining: tender fava beans simmered low and slow, crushed with fragrant cumin and garlic, and enriched with sesame oil and salted white cheese.

Prep Time 15 mins
Cook Time 1 hr 30 mins
Total Time 1 hr 45 mins
Servings 4 servings
Cultural Significance

A Breakfast Ritual Across the Nile Valley

In Sudan, ful medames is far more than an everyday meal—it is a communal gathering ritual. While fava bean preparation has ancient roots spanning the Nile, the Sudanese method is distinctively flavorful. Cooked slowly in heavy copper or earthenware pots called qidra, the beans are partially mashed and dressed with cold-pressed sesame oil (zeet simsim), toasted ground cumin, minced garlic, crumbled salted sheep's milk cheese (gibna bayda), and sliced boiled eggs. Served family-style in large communal bowls, it is eaten enthusiastically using warm crusty bread as an edible utensil.

Ingredients

Measured for 4 hearty servings.

  • Dry small fava beans (soaked overnight) 2 cups
  • Red lentils (for rich broth thickening) ¼ cup
  • Garlic cloves, finely minced 4 cloves
  • Freshly roasted ground cumin 1½ tbsp
  • Fresh lemon juice 3 tbsp
  • Pure unrefined sesame oil (or extra virgin olive oil) ⅓ cup
  • Crumbled Sudanese white cheese (gibna bayda or feta) 100 g
  • Hard-boiled eggs, quartered 2 eggs
  • Fresh ripe tomato, diced 1 medium
  • Fresh parsley or coriander, chopped ¼ cup
  • Sea salt and black pepper to taste
  • Warm Sudanese flatbread or crusty rolls for serving

Preparation Instructions

Click each step to expand details and cooking notes.

Step 1: Soak & Prepare the Fava Beans

Rinse the dried fava beans thoroughly and soak them in ample cold water for at least 8–12 hours. Drain and rinse the soaked beans before cooking. Rinse the red lentils separately in cold water.

Step 2: Slow Simmer to Creamy Tenderness

Transfer the soaked fava beans and red lentils into a heavy pot or Dutch oven. Cover with fresh water by at least 3 inches above the beans. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, skimming off any foam. Once boiling, reduce the heat to the lowest possible setting, cover with a tight-fitting lid, and simmer gently for 1½ to 2 hours until the beans are completely tender and can be easily mashed between your fingers. Keep hot water nearby to top up if the pot becomes dry.

Step 3: Lightly Crush & Season

Once tender, use a potato masher or the back of a large wooden spoon to crush approximately half of the beans directly in the pot. This creates the classic contrast between whole soft beans and a velvety, starchy sauce. Stir in the minced garlic, ground cumin, and sea salt. Simmer uncovered for 5 minutes to meld the aromatics.

Step 4: Garnish & Dress the Sudanese Way

Ladle the hot ful into wide, shallow serving bowls. Squeeze fresh lemon juice across the top. Generously drizzle with golden toasted sesame oil. Scatter diced fresh tomatoes, crumbled gibna bayda cheese, quartered boiled eggs, and finely chopped parsley across the surface.

Step 5: Serve Communally

Serve immediately alongside warm crusty bread or hearth-baked flatbreads. Guests tear pieces of bread and scoop the seasoned beans directly from the plate, catching garnishes in each bite.
